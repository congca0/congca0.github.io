# Research

My research develops statistical and machine learning methods for complex
biomedical and population health problems. I work at the intersection of
causal inference, machine learning, and biomedical data science, with an
emphasis on developing methods that are interpretable and useful for
scientific and clinical decision-making.

My research has evolved from machine learning for environmental and
population health toward causal and multimodal methods for biomedical
research.

---

## Causal AI and Clinical Decision-Making

I develop causal inference and machine learning methods for studying
heterogeneous treatment response and supporting individualized clinical
decision-making.

A central focus of this work is developing statistical methods that move
beyond prediction and association toward questions about interventions,
treatment effects, and clinical decision-making. I am particularly interested
in methods that can incorporate rich patient-level information while
maintaining a rigorous causal framework.

### Selected projects

**Individualized Treatment-Effect Estimation**

I develop causal machine learning methods for estimating heterogeneous
treatment effects using large-scale clinical and real-world data. This work
focuses on understanding how treatment effects vary across patients and how
such heterogeneity can be characterized using high-dimensional patient
information.

**Causal Inference under Interference**

I study causal inference when an individual's outcome may depend on
treatments received by other individuals. My work focuses on causal
estimation when the relevant exposure structure is uncertain and may need
to be learned from complex data.

## Multimodal Representation Learning for Biomedical Data

Biomedical datasets increasingly combine information from multiple domains,
including clinical characteristics, laboratory measurements, behavioral
factors, socioeconomic conditions, and genomic information.

I develop representation learning methods that integrate heterogeneous
biomedical data into interpretable latent representations. The goal is to
capture complex structure across data modalities while retaining meaningful
relationships with health outcomes.

### Selected project

**AI-driven Multimodal Representation Learning for Latent Mediation
Structure Discovery**

I developed a multimodal representation learning framework for identifying
latent structures across socioeconomic, psychosocial, clinical, laboratory,
behavioral, and genomic data. The framework combines modality-specific
representation learning with causal mediation analysis to study complex
relationships associated with cardiometabolic outcomes.

## Machine Learning for Environmental and Population Health

My earlier research developed machine learning and statistical methods for
understanding complex environmental systems and their relationships with
population health.

Using large-scale longitudinal data, I studied interactions among air
pollution, weather, traffic, climate, and health outcomes. This work
included the development of longitudinal environmental data resources and
machine learning approaches for nonlinear and time-varying exposure-response
relationships.

### Selected projects

**Environmental Exposure and Population Health**

I developed machine learning methods to characterize the relationships among
traffic, air pollution, weather conditions, and cardiopulmonary health
outcomes using large-scale longitudinal data from Norway.

**Physics-Informed Machine Learning**

I developed machine learning approaches that incorporate scientific and
physical knowledge into environmental prediction models, with applications
to urban air pollution and environmental systems.

**Longitudinal Environmental Data Integration**

I developed a longitudinal data resource integrating ten years of daily
weather, traffic, and air pollution data across six major Norwegian cities.

## Research Program

Across these projects, my research combines **causal inference,
statistical machine learning, and multimodal data analysis** to develop
methods for complex scientific and biomedical problems.

My long-term goal is to develop rigorous and interpretable computational
methods that can connect heterogeneous data with actionable evidence for
health and clinical research.
